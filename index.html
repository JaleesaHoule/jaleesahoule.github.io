Gust response in free flight

2024 – 2026 · published study

How do flies respond to getting blown off course while performing a task? I ran optogenetically triggered wind-tunnel experiments with multi-camera tracking, created a moving-mesh CFD model of our wind tunnel setup, and merged the wind and trajectory streams into one dataset — showing that freely flying flies keep track of wind history when performing search tasks.

Outdoor odor-tracking study

2023 – 2025 · published study

I modified commerical insect traps with custom firmware to track insect arrival events, then deployed them across farm, forest, and urban sites, and logged arrivals alongside 10 Hz wind measurements for six continuous months. I then parsed months of data for insight into the atmospheric conditions yellowjackets are most likely to locate odors in.

A little about me

I decided to hike the Appalachian Trail when I was 20 and unsure of what to do with my life. I fell in love with moving through nature, and subsequently decided to hike the Pacific Crest Trail (2016), Te Araroa (2016-17), and Continental Divide Trail (2018). I've worked in a remote hut in the White Mountains, spent a year as a crew leader for Conservation Corps, and was on a working holiday visa in Australia during COVID. I returned to school in 2021 to pursue a graduate degree in engineering, where I fell in love with research. I still don't entirely know where my path is leading in life, but am figuring it out while I make it. I sometimes run far, climb, splitboard, and dabble in making sourdough.
